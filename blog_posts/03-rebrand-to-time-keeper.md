# From AgeClock to Time Keeper — Giving the App Its Identity

**Date:** 4 - 5 February 2026
**Phase:** Branding and Identity
**Tags:** Branding, UX Design, Android Resources, Product Thinking

---

## Why a Rebrand?

The project started under the working title "AgeClock" — functional but forgettable. As the app matured and its purpose became clearer, the name didn't capture what it was really about. It wasn't just a clock. It wasn't just about age. It was about **keeping time** with the people who matter.

**Time Keeper** better conveys the emotional intent of the app: a quiet, personal tool for being mindful of the time we share with the people in our lives.

## What Changed

### App Identity

- **App name** updated across the Android manifest, strings resources, and all user-facing surfaces.
- **Package references** and internal naming conventions reviewed to ensure consistency.
- **Theme and colour palette** refined to align with the new identity — a cleaner, more purposeful visual language.

### Branding Assets

- New branding elements introduced to give the app a cohesive look and feel, both within the app and in preparation for eventual store listing.
- The shift in tone from "utility" to "meaningful personal tool" influenced design decisions going forward.

## Skills Used

- **Android resource management** — updating strings, themes, and manifest entries across the project in a way that's clean and doesn't leave orphaned references.
- **Product thinking** — stepping back from code to consider what the app communicates to the user. A name is the first thing a user encounters; it sets expectations.
- **Branding consistency** — ensuring the name change propagated correctly through all layers: UI strings, widget labels, app launcher text, and internal documentation.

## What This Means for the User

The user now sees an app called "Time Keeper" on their device — a name that immediately communicates purpose. It's a small change in terms of code, but a significant one in terms of how the app is perceived. First impressions matter, especially on a crowded app launcher.

## Reflection

Naming is one of those tasks developers often postpone ("we'll figure out the name later"), but it shapes every decision that follows — store listings, marketing copy, visual design, even how you talk about the project. Getting it right early, before the app goes public, saved rework later.

The rebrand also served as a forcing function to audit the entire project for consistency, which caught a few stale references and improved the overall code hygiene.
